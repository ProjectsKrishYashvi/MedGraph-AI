KDIGO 2024 Clinical Practice Guidelines
Chronic Kidney Disease: Evaluation and Management
Source: Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) — Adapted for MedGraph AI RAG Subsystem

=============================================================================
SECTION 1: CKD DEFINITION AND STAGING
=============================================================================

Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) is defined as abnormalities of kidney structure or function, present for more than 3 months, with implications for health.

CKD is confirmed by any of the following markers present for > 3 months:
- Glomerular filtration rate (GFR) < 60 mL/min/1.73m2 (GFR categories G3a-G5)
- Markers of kidney damage: Albuminuria (AER >= 30 mg/24h, ACR >= 30 mg/g), urine sediment abnormalities, electrolyte and other disorders due to tubular disorders, histological abnormalities, structural abnormalities by imaging, history of kidney transplantation

GFR Categories (G1-G5):
- G1: GFR >= 90 mL/min/1.73m2 — Normal or high (CKD if kidney damage markers present)
- G2: GFR 60-89 mL/min/1.73m2 — Mildly decreased
- G3a: GFR 45-59 mL/min/1.73m2 — Mildly to moderately decreased
- G3b: GFR 30-44 mL/min/1.73m2 — Moderately to severely decreased
- G4: GFR 15-29 mL/min/1.73m2 — Severely decreased
- G5: GFR < 15 mL/min/1.73m2 — Kidney failure (dialysis or transplant required)

Albuminuria Categories (A1-A3):
- A1: ACR < 30 mg/g — Normal to mildly increased
- A2: ACR 30-300 mg/g — Moderately increased (formerly "microalbuminuria")
- A3: ACR > 300 mg/g — Severely increased (formerly "macroalbuminuria")

Risk stratification: CKD risk is jointly determined by GFR category and albuminuria category. High and very high-risk categories require more frequent monitoring and aggressive management.

SECTION 2: CKD — CAUSES AND SCREENING
=============================================================================

Most common causes of CKD globally:
1. Diabetic kidney disease (diabetic nephropathy) — accounts for approximately 30-40% of all CKD cases
2. Hypertensive nephrosclerosis — approximately 25% of CKD cases
3. Glomerulonephritis — approximately 10-15% of CKD cases
4. Polycystic kidney disease (PKD)
5. Recurrent urinary tract infections / pyelonephritis
6. Obstructive uropathy
7. Drug-induced nephrotoxicity (NSAIDs, aminoglycosides, contrast agents)

Screening recommendations:
- Annual screening with eGFR and urine ACR for ALL patients with:
  - Diabetes mellitus (type 1 or type 2)
  - Hypertension
  - Age >= 60 years
  - Family history of CKD or hereditary kidney disease
  - History of acute kidney injury (AKI)
  - Obesity (BMI >= 30 kg/m2)
  - Cardiovascular disease

SECTION 3: CKD — BLOOD PRESSURE MANAGEMENT
=============================================================================

Blood pressure targets in CKD:
- CKD with albuminuria (ACR >= 30 mg/g): Target SBP < 120 mmHg (intensive BP control; based on SPRINT and ACCORD trials)
- CKD without albuminuria: Target SBP < 130 mmHg
- CKD with diabetes: Target < 130/80 mmHg

Preferred antihypertensive agents in CKD:
1. ACE inhibitors (e.g., lisinopril, ramipril, enalapril):
   - First-choice in CKD with albuminuria (ACR >= 30 mg/g)
   - Reduce proteinuria by 30-40% beyond BP-lowering effects
   - Caution: May worsen hyperkalemia; monitor potassium and creatinine at 1-2 weeks after initiation or dose increase
   - Do not use if eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m2 unless specialist guidance available
   
2. ARBs (e.g., losartan, irbesartan, valsartan):
   - Equivalent renoprotective efficacy to ACE inhibitors
   - Preferred when ACE inhibitor cough is intolerable
   - Do NOT combine ACE inhibitor + ARB (dual RAAS blockade increases risk of AKI and hyperkalemia without additional benefit)
   
3. Dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers (e.g., amlodipine):
   - Safe and effective add-on in CKD
   - Less effective at reducing proteinuria than ACE inhibitors/ARBs when used alone
   
4. Loop diuretics (e.g., furosemide):
   - Preferred over thiazides when eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m2 (thiazides are less effective at low GFR)
   - Essential for managing volume overload and edema in advanced CKD

SECTION 4: DIABETIC KIDNEY DISEASE — MANAGEMENT
=============================================================================

Diabetic kidney disease (DKD) is the leading cause of CKD worldwide. Optimal management requires:

Glycemic control:
- HbA1c target: 7.0% (53 mmol/mol) for most patients with DKD
- More lenient target (HbA1c < 8.0%) for elderly, frail patients, or those with high hypoglycemia risk
- Avoid sulfonylureas (especially glyburide/glibenclamide) in advanced CKD — high risk of hypoglycemia
- Insulin dose requirements decrease as kidney function declines

SGLT-2 inhibitors in DKD:
- Empagliflozin, canagliflozin, dapagliflozin are recommended for patients with T2DM and CKD (eGFR >= 20 mL/min/1.73m2)
- Benefits: Slows CKD progression (reduces risk of 40% decline in eGFR, kidney failure, or death from kidney causes by 30-40%)
- Reduces hospitalizations for heart failure
- Mechanism of renoprotection: Tubuloglomerular feedback, reduced intraglomerular pressure, anti-fibrotic effects

GLP-1 receptor agonists in DKD:
- Semaglutide and liraglutide have demonstrated reduction in new-onset albuminuria and progression of DKD
- Weight reduction benefits further improve cardiorenal outcomes

Finerenone (non-steroidal MRA):
- Indicated for CKD associated with T2DM (eGFR >= 25 mL/min/1.73m2, ACR >= 30 mg/g, serum potassium <= 4.8 mEq/L)
- Reduces risk of CKD progression and cardiovascular events

RAAS blockade:
- ACE inhibitor or ARB (NOT both) is recommended for all patients with DKD and albuminuria
- Reduces progression from microalbuminuria to macroalbuminuria
- Reduces progression to ESKD by approximately 16-25% in patients with overt DKD

SECTION 5: CKD — CARDIOVASCULAR RISK MANAGEMENT
=============================================================================

Patients with CKD have markedly elevated cardiovascular risk. CVD is the leading cause of death in CKD patients, even before reaching kidney failure.

Lipid management:
- Statin therapy is recommended for all adults with CKD aged >= 50 years
- High-intensity statin (atorvastatin 40-80 mg) for patients with established CVD
- Moderate-intensity statin for patients without established CVD but with CKD
- Statins do NOT slow GFR decline but significantly reduce cardiovascular events and mortality
- Do not initiate statins in dialysis patients without prior indication (SHARP trial evidence)
- Avoid dose-dependent statins: adjust dose for GFR (e.g., rosuvastatin dose cap in severe CKD)

Antiplatelet therapy:
- Aspirin (75-100 mg/day) for secondary prevention of CVD in CKD patients with established CVD
- Risk-benefit analysis required: CKD patients have both elevated cardiovascular risk AND elevated bleeding risk

Anemia management:
- Target hemoglobin: 10-12 g/dL for patients on erythropoiesis-stimulating agents (ESAs)
- Iron supplementation: IV iron preferred over oral iron in dialysis patients (poor oral absorption)
- Erythropoiesis-stimulating agents (ESAs): Indicated when Hgb < 10 g/dL AND iron stores are adequate (ferritin > 200 ng/mL, TSAT > 20%)
- Avoid targeting Hgb > 13 g/dL with ESAs (increased risk of stroke and cardiovascular events)

SECTION 6: CKD — MEDICATION DOSE ADJUSTMENTS
=============================================================================

Critical medications requiring dose adjustment in CKD:

Antibiotics:
- Penicillins: Reduce dose in GFR < 30 mL/min
- Aminoglycosides: Avoid if possible; nephrotoxic, ototoxic. If necessary, monitor levels closely
- Nitrofurantoin: Contraindicated in GFR < 30 mL/min (risk of peripheral neuropathy)
- Fluoroquinolones: Dose reduction required in GFR < 30 mL/min

Diabetes medications:
- Metformin: Contraindicated in eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m2; dose reduce if eGFR 30-45 mL/min/1.73m2
- Sulfonylureas: Avoid glyburide in CKD; use glipizide with caution (GFR > 30); consider dose reduction
- SGLT-2 inhibitors: Reduce glucose-lowering efficacy at GFR < 30; empagliflozin/dapagliflozin may continue for cardiorenal benefit at eGFR >= 20
- DPP-4 inhibitors: Linagliptin requires NO dose adjustment in CKD; sitagliptin/saxagliptin require dose reduction

Cardiovascular medications:
- Digoxin: Narrow therapeutic index; significant dose reduction required in CKD; monitor levels
- Atenolol: Renally cleared; dose reduce in GFR < 35 mL/min
- Spironolactone: Use with extreme caution in CKD (risk of life-threatening hyperkalemia); avoid if eGFR < 30

Analgesics (extremely important):
- NSAIDs (e.g., ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac): AVOID in CKD — cause acute vasoconstriction, reduce GFR, worsen proteinuria, increase risk of AKI
- Opioids: Reduce dose; active metabolites accumulate in renal failure
- Paracetamol (acetaminophen): Preferred analgesic in CKD at usual doses

=============================================================================
SECTION 7: CKD — DIETARY RECOMMENDATIONS
=============================================================================

Dietary modifications in CKD:

Protein:
- CKD stages G3-G5 (not on dialysis): Reduce protein intake to 0.6-0.8 g/kg/day
- Dialysis patients: Increase protein to 1.0-1.2 g/kg/day to compensate for dialytic losses
- Avoid very high protein diets (> 1.3 g/kg/day) in CKD — accelerates GFR decline

Sodium:
- Target < 2 g/day sodium (< 5 g/day salt) for all CKD patients
- Reduces blood pressure and proteinuria

Potassium:
- Restriction required in hyperkalemia (serum potassium > 5.5 mEq/L)
- Avoid potassium-rich foods: bananas, oranges, potatoes, tomatoes, avocado, dried fruits
- Cooking (boiling and discarding water) reduces vegetable potassium content by 30-50%

Phosphate:
- Restriction required in hyperphosphatemia (serum phosphate > 4.5 mg/dL in CKD stages G3-G4; > 5.5 mg/dL in G5)
- Avoid processed foods, dark colas, dairy excess
- Phosphate binders (calcium carbonate, sevelamer) taken with meals to reduce GI absorption

SECTION 8: CKD — MONITORING SCHEDULE
=============================================================================

Monitoring frequency based on CKD stage and risk:

Low risk (G1-G2, A1): Annual monitoring of eGFR and ACR
Moderate risk (G3a-G3b, A1; G1-G2, A2): Every 6 months
High risk (G3a-G3b, A2; G4, A1): Every 3-4 months
Very high risk (G4-G5 or A3): Every 1-3 months

Laboratory monitoring panel:
- eGFR (calculated from serum creatinine using CKD-EPI equation)
- Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (ACR)
- Serum potassium (especially on ACE inhibitor/ARB or SGLT-2 inhibitor)
- Serum bicarbonate (metabolic acidosis is common in CKD G4-G5)
- Hemoglobin (anemia surveillance)
- Serum phosphate and PTH (mineral and bone disorder)
- Blood pressure at every visit

Referral to nephrology:
- eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m2 (CKD G4-G5)
- Rapid decline in eGFR (> 5 mL/min/1.73m2 per year)
- ACR > 300 mg/g (persistent macroalbuminuria)
- Resistant hypertension
- Suspected glomerulonephritis or hereditary kidney disease
