SECTION 1: DIABETES COMPREHENSIVE CARE — OVERVIEW
=============================================================================

The goals of diabetes management are:
1. Prevent or delay the development of complications
2. Treat existing complications
3. Improve quality of life and reduce morbidity and mortality

The foundation of diabetes care includes:
- Glycemic management (HbA1c, time-in-range, fasting and postprandial glucose)
- Blood pressure control
- Lipid management
- Weight management
- Smoking cessation
- Physical activity promotion
- Self-management education and support (DSMES)
- Psychological care and screening for diabetes distress

Team-based care: Optimal diabetes management requires a multidisciplinary team including primary care physician, endocrinologist, certified diabetes care and education specialist (CDCES), registered dietitian nutritionist (RDN), pharmacist, and mental health professional.

SECTION 2: GLYCEMIC MONITORING AND TECHNOLOGY
=============================================================================

Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c):
- Reflects average blood glucose over the preceding 2-3 months
- Test at least twice per year in stable patients; every 3 months when therapy is changed or targets not met
- Point-of-care HbA1c testing: Acceptable with NGSP-certified methods (results available within minutes at clinic visit)
- Limitations: Inaccurate in hemolytic anemia, hemoglobinopathies (sickle cell disease, thalassemia); affected by iron deficiency, pregnancy

Time-in-Range (TIR):
- Glucose Management Indicator (GMI): Estimated HbA1c from CGM data
- TIR target (70-180 mg/dL): > 70% of time for most adults with T1DM or T2DM on insulin
- Time Below Range (TBR) target (< 70 mg/dL): < 4% of time
- Time Above Range (TAR) target (> 180 mg/dL): < 25% of time

Self-monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG):
- Recommended for patients on insulin regimens to detect asymptomatic hypoglycemia
- For patients on oral agents only: Less evidence for benefit; consider situational monitoring (illness, symptom assessment)

SECTION 3: OBESITY AND WEIGHT MANAGEMENT IN DIABETES
=============================================================================

Weight management targets:
- Weight loss of >= 5% reduces HbA1c, triglycerides, blood pressure, and reduces need for diabetes medications
- Weight loss of >= 10-15% may lead to partial or complete diabetes remission in patients with T2DM of shorter duration (< 10 years)
- Sustained weight loss of >= 15-20%: Associated with long-term diabetes remission (LOOK AHEAD, DiRECT trials)

Pharmacotherapy for obesity in patients with T2DM:
- GLP-1 receptor agonists: Semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy), liraglutide (Victoza/Saxenda) — 5-15% weight reduction
- GIP/GLP-1 dual agonist: Tirzepatide (Mounjaro) — up to 20-22% weight reduction (SURMOUNT trials)
- Consider anti-obesity medications when BMI >= 30 kg/m2, or >= 27 kg/m2 with weight-related comorbidities

Metabolic/bariatric surgery:
- Recommended for adults with T2DM and BMI >= 40 kg/m2 (regardless of glycemic control)
- Recommended for adults with T2DM and BMI 35-39.9 kg/m2 when glycemic control is inadequate despite optimal medical therapy
- May be considered in BMI 30-34.9 kg/m2 with inadequate glycemic control
- Procedures: Roux-en-Y gastric bypass, sleeve gastrectomy; bypass procedures have superior glycemic outcomes

SECTION 4: HYPERTENSION MANAGEMENT IN DIABETES
=============================================================================

Blood pressure targets in diabetes:
- Most adults with diabetes and hypertension: Target < 130/80 mmHg
- Older adults (>= 65 years) or with cardiovascular disease: Target < 130/80 mmHg (if tolerated)
- Avoid target SBP < 120 mmHg in older adults with diabetes (increased risk of adverse events)

Measurement: Confirmed with multiple measurements on multiple occasions; use validated device with appropriate cuff size.

Lifestyle interventions for hypertension in diabetes:
- DASH dietary pattern: Reduce SBP by 5-11 mmHg
- Sodium restriction (< 2.3 g/day): Reduces SBP by 3-5 mmHg
- Weight loss (5-10% body weight): Reduces SBP by 5-20 mmHg per 10 kg lost
- Aerobic exercise: 30 min/day, 5 days/week — reduces SBP by 5-8 mmHg
- Alcohol moderation

Pharmacological treatment:
- Start pharmacotherapy when SBP >= 130 mmHg confirmed on two occasions
- First-line agents: ACE inhibitor OR ARB (NOT both) — particularly indicated in DKD (albuminuria)
- If ACE inhibitor/ARB cannot be used: amlodipine (CCB) or thiazide-like diuretic
- Most patients require two or more agents to achieve < 130/80 mmHg target
- Once-daily regimens and fixed-dose combinations improve adherence

SECTION 5: DYSLIPIDEMIA MANAGEMENT IN DIABETES
=============================================================================

Lipid screening: Obtain fasting lipid profile at initial diabetes evaluation, at age 10 (for children with T2DM), and periodically (every 5 years, or more frequently if indicated).

Statin therapy — primary prevention:
- Age 40-75 years with T2DM: Moderate-intensity statin therapy regardless of LDL level
- Age < 40 years: Consider moderate-intensity statin if LDL > 100 mg/dL or other CVD risk factors
- Age > 75 years: Individualize decision; continue statin if already tolerated

Statin therapy — secondary prevention (established ASCVD):
- High-intensity statin: Atorvastatin 40-80 mg/day or rosuvastatin 20-40 mg/day
- Target LDL-C: < 70 mg/dL (< 1.8 mmol/L); consider < 55 mg/dL for very high-risk patients

Add-on non-statin therapy (when LDL-C remains above target on maximum tolerated statin):
- Ezetimibe: Reduces LDL-C by additional 15-20%; proven CVD event reduction (IMPROVE-IT trial)
- PCSK9 inhibitors (alirocumab, evolocumab): Reduces LDL-C by 50-60%; consider in very high-risk patients

Triglyceride management:
- Lifestyle modification (reduce refined carbohydrates, alcohol, added sugars): First-line
- Triglycerides 500-1000 mg/dL: Fibrate therapy (fenofibrate) to reduce pancreatitis risk
- Omega-3 fatty acids (icosapentaenoic acid, EPA): Vascepa (4 g/day) reduces cardiovascular events in patients with elevated triglycerides (>= 150 mg/dL) on statin therapy (REDUCE-IT trial)

SECTION 6: CARDIOVASCULAR DISEASE AND DIABETES
=============================================================================

Cardiovascular risk assessment:
- Adults with T2DM are at substantially elevated risk of ASCVD (coronary artery disease, stroke, peripheral arterial disease)
- Risk calculators: AHA/ACC Pooled Cohort Equations, UKPDS Risk Engine (diabetes-specific)
- 10-year ASCVD risk > 10%: High-risk category requiring aggressive intervention

Cardioprotective medications (recommended for patients with T2DM and established CVD, heart failure, or high CVD risk):

SGLT-2 inhibitors (empagliflozin, canagliflozin, dapagliflozin):
- Established ASCVD or high CVD risk: Reduce major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) by 11-14% (EMPA-REG, CANVAS, DECLARE trials)
- Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF): Reduce hospitalizations for heart failure by 30-35% regardless of diabetes status
- CKD: Reduces kidney disease progression (DAPA-CKD, CREDENCE trials)

GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide, liraglutide, dulaglutide):
- Established ASCVD: Reduce MACE risk by 12-26% (LEADER, SUSTAIN-6, REWIND trials)
- Weight loss benefits (3-5 kg on average, up to 15% with high-dose semaglutide)
- Not recommended in patients with personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN2

Aspirin therapy:
- Secondary prevention (established ASCVD): Low-dose aspirin 75-100 mg/day recommended
- Primary prevention: Consider in high CVD risk patients (10-year risk > 10%) without high bleeding risk; increasing evidence against routine use for primary prevention

SECTION 7: CHRONIC KIDNEY DISEASE AND DIABETES
=============================================================================

Screening for diabetic kidney disease:
- Begin DKD screening at T2DM diagnosis (CKD may already be present at time of T2DM diagnosis)
- Annual eGFR and urinary ACR for all patients with T2DM

Staging of albuminuria in T2DM:
- Normal: ACR < 30 mg/g — Annual monitoring
- Mildly elevated (microalbuminuria): ACR 30-299 mg/g — RAAS blockade with ACE inhibitor or ARB
- Severely elevated (macroalbuminuria): ACR >= 300 mg/g — Intensify RAAS blockade, add SGLT-2 inhibitor

Preventing CKD progression in T2DM:
1. ACE inhibitor or ARB (not both) — reduces proteinuria by 30-40%, slows GFR decline
2. SGLT-2 inhibitor — additional 30-40% relative risk reduction in CKD progression (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin)
3. Optimize glycemic control: Every 1% reduction in HbA1c reduces microvascular complications (including nephropathy) by 37%
4. Strict blood pressure control: < 130/80 mmHg
5. Avoid nephrotoxic agents: NSAIDs, IV contrast (use with caution, ensure adequate hydration and eGFR monitoring), aminoglycosides

Medication considerations in advanced DKD:
- Discontinue metformin when eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m2
- SGLT-2 inhibitors: Continue at eGFR >= 20 for cardiorenal protection (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin); glycemic efficacy diminished at eGFR < 30
- Insulin dose reduction required as GFR declines (kidney is responsible for 30-40% of insulin clearance)
- Sulfonylureas: Avoid or use extreme caution in GFR < 30; prefer glipizide with lowest effective dose

SECTION 8: DIABETES IN OLDER ADULTS
=============================================================================

Considerations for diabetes management in patients aged >= 65 years:

Glycemic targets:
- Healthy older adults (few coexisting chronic conditions, intact cognitive and functional status): HbA1c < 7.0-7.5%
- Complex/intermediate health status (multiple coexisting conditions, cognitive impairment or functional dependence): HbA1c < 8.0%
- Very complex/poor health (long-term care residents, end-stage disease, frailty): HbA1c < 8.5%; avoid symptomatic hyperglycemia

Hypoglycemia risk in older adults:
- Older adults are at substantially higher risk of severe hypoglycemia and its consequences (falls, fractures, cognitive decline, cardiovascular events, hospitalization)
- Prefer hypoglycemia-safe medications: GLP-1 agonists, DPP-4 inhibitors, SGLT-2 inhibitors
- Avoid or use extreme caution with sulfonylureas (especially glyburide) and insulin regimens in frail elderly

Deprescribing in older adults with diabetes:
- Consider relaxing glycemic targets and simplifying regimens in patients with limited life expectancy, severe hypoglycemia unawareness, or complex care needs
- Reduce polypharmacy: Identify and discontinue low-value medications

Cognitive decline and dementia:
- T2DM is independently associated with 1.5-2x increased risk of dementia
- Optimize cardiovascular risk factors to reduce dementia risk
- Simplify medication regimens in cognitively impaired patients; avoid hypoglycemia-prone medications

SECTION 9: DIABETES — SELF-MANAGEMENT EDUCATION
=============================================================================

Diabetes Self-Management Education and Support (DSMES):
- All patients with diabetes should receive DSMES at diagnosis and regularly thereafter
- Four critical time points for DSMES referral:
  1. At diagnosis
  2. Annually or when not meeting treatment goals
  3. When complicating factors develop (medical, physical, psychosocial)
  4. When transitions in care occur

Key DSMES topics:
- Healthy eating and meal planning
- Physical activity and its effects on blood glucose
- Medication management and adherence
- Blood glucose monitoring and pattern management
- Sick day management
- Prevention, detection, and treatment of hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia
- Foot care and daily foot inspection
- Diabetes distress, depression, and mental health

Physical activity recommendations:
- Aerobic activity: >= 150 minutes/week of moderate-intensity, OR >= 75 minutes/week vigorous-intensity; avoid > 2 consecutive days without exercise
- Resistance training: >= 2-3 sessions per week on non-consecutive days
- Sedentary time: Break up prolonged sitting every 30 minutes; prolonged sitting independently associated with hyperglycemia
- Older adults: Add flexibility and balance training to reduce fall risk

SECTION 10: PREVENTIVE CARE AND IMMUNIZATIONS IN DIABETES
=============================================================================

Annual care checklist for patients with T2DM:
- HbA1c: Every 3 months (if not at goal) or every 6 months (if stable at goal)
- Blood pressure: At every visit
- Lipid panel: At least annually (more frequently if not at goal)
- Urine ACR: Annually
- eGFR: Annually
- Foot examination: Comprehensive annual foot exam; visual inspection at every visit
- Dilated eye exam: Annual ophthalmology referral for diabetic retinopathy screening
- Dental exam: Every 6 months (periodontal disease associated with poor glycemic control)
- Depression/diabetes distress screening: At diagnosis and periodically (PHQ-9 or Problem Areas in Diabetes scale)

Immunizations:
- Influenza vaccine: Annually
- Pneumococcal vaccine (PCV20 or PPSV23): Per age-based recommendations
- Hepatitis B vaccine: Unvaccinated adults < 60 years with T2DM; consider ages >= 60 years
- COVID-19 vaccine: Per current public health recommendations
- Zoster vaccine (Shingrix): Adults >= 50 years with T2DM (high risk for herpes zoster complications)

Neuropathy screening:
- Comprehensive foot examination including 10-g monofilament testing and vibration testing (128-Hz tuning fork) annually
- Diabetic peripheral neuropathy affects up to 50% of patients with long-duration T2DM
- Treatment: Pregabalin, duloxetine, or gabapentin for painful diabetic neuropathy (symptomatic relief, not disease-modifying)
